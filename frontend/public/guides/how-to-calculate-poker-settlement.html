How to Calculate Poker Settlement & Net Player Winnings

Learn how to calculate player balances, reconcile cash-out stacks with buy-ins, and use debt minimization algorithms to settle Texas Hold'em cash games effortlessly.

1. The Fundamentals of Cash Game Accounting

In a home poker cash game, chips have direct monetary value. When the host calls "last hand," the accounting process begins:

  • Step A: Count each player's physical chip stack by denomination.
  • Step B: Multiply each chip count by its assigned value to determine total cash-out value.
  • Step C: Subtract total buy-in cash (initial buy-in + rebuys) from cash-out value.

2. Verifying the Zero-Sum Condition

Before any money changes hands, verify the golden rule of poker math:

Table Balance Invariant
Σ (All Buy-In Money) ≡ Σ (All Cash-Out Chip Values)

If there is an imbalance, common causes include:

  • A rebuy that was handed to a player but never written down.
  • Chips knocked off the table onto the floor.
  • A player miscounting their stack during cash-out.

3. Resolving Debts with Greedy Bipartite Matching

Consider a game with 4 players: Alice (+₹3,000), Bob (+₹1,000), Charlie (-₹2,500), and David (-₹1,500). Instead of Charlie paying Alice ₹2,500, David paying Alice ₹500, and David paying Bob ₹1,000, ChipMate's algorithm computes:

  • Charlie pays Alice ₹2,500
  • David pays Bob ₹1,000
  • David pays Alice ₹500

This ensures everyone receives exactly their winnings with zero intermediate accounts.
